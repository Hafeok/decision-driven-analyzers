### #107: DD0019 reads

`HeldCollectionReads` classifies a call on a held collection as a read in two cases:

- (a) the method implements a member of `IReadOnlyCollection<T>`, `IReadOnlyList<T>`,
  `IReadOnlyDictionary<K,V>` or `IReadOnlySet<T>` that the field's type implements, found through
  `FindImplementationForInterfaceMember`, or the field is typed as one of those interfaces;
- (b) it is `Contains` on a field typed `HashSet<T>`/`ISet<T>`, or `ContainsKey`, `ContainsValue`,
  `IndexOf`, `GetEnumerator` or `TryGetValue`, and in every case declared in `System.Collections`,
  `.Generic` or `.ObjectModel`.

The issue's two examples (`Scope`, `Table`) are pinned as conforming rows. `Add`, `Remove`,
`Clear`, an indexer set and `TryAdd` are pinned as violating rows. With the classification switched
off, 12 of the 15 conforming rows fail. The other three (indexer get, `Count`, `foreach`) are
property reads or enumeration, which the body check never treated as calls.

The samples job carries a read-only model class (`Origins`) beside DD0019's violating sample.
Against the previous analyzer, `check-violations.sh` fails with "DD0019 was reported 2 times".

## Questions

1. **The delegate arm.** The prompt ruled that "calls through a delegate or an unknown interface
   still report". Before #110, DD0019 did not see a held collection's method group passed as a
   delegate at all (`more.Where(items.Add)`). Invoking a delegate *field* was already excluded by
   the #47 decision, and that test was kept. The ruling was read as the method-group case and
   implemented, which is a narrow new finding and is listed as breaking in the changelog. If the
   ruling meant "no change from today", the method-group arm is the six lines in
   `MutableModelAnalyzer.Mutation` plus one test row, and can be taken out with a new version of the key.
2. **`List<T>.Contains` and `ICollection<T>.Contains`.** Neither is covered by (a), because no
   read-only interface declares `Contains`, or by (b), which names `Contains` only on
   `HashSet<T>`/`ISet<T>`. So `items.Contains(x)` on a held `List<T>` still reports, and a test pins
   that. It is a read. Options: (i) keep it as is, since the remedy is to hold an `IReadOnlyList<T>`
   or `ImmutableArray<T>`; (ii) widen (b) to `Contains` on any `System.Collections*` type; (iii)
   add other common reads (`Exists`, `Find`, `CopyTo`, `ToArray`). Each needs a new version of
   `DomainModelImmutable`.
